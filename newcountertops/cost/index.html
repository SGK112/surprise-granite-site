What actually moves the price

Material is the headline, but it is rarely why a quote comes back higher than expected.

Edges. A standard eased or square edge is included. A mitred or waterfall edge means fabricating a second face and matching the veining across the join — on an island that can add a four-figure sum on its own.

Cutouts. An undermount sink, a cooktop, a bar prep sink and faucet holes each carry labour. Most kitchens have two or three.

Removing what's there. Tear-out and disposal of the old tops is separate work, and tile or laminate bonded to the cabinets takes longer than a slab does.

Plumbing. Disconnecting and reconnecting a sink and disposal is a trade visit, not something the countertop crew does on the way out.

Common questions

How much do new countertops cost?

Installed, most kitchens land between $64 and $140 per square foot depending on material. A typical 45 square foot kitchen is roughly $2,902 to $6,300. Material is the variable; fabrication and installation run about $55 per square foot on top of it.

What is the cheapest countertop material?

Granite, by a margin — it starts around $9 per square foot for material where quartz starts nearer $14. Natural stone being cheaper than engineered stone surprises people, but granite is quarried in volume and quartz is manufactured.

Does that price include installation?

The installed figures on this page include fabrication and installation at $55 per square foot — cutting the slab, the sink cutout, the edge profile, delivery and fitting. They do not include removing your old countertops, plumbing reconnection, or tile backsplash.

Why is my quote higher than these numbers?

Usually edges and extras. A waterfall edge adds a whole slab side, a mitred edge doubles the fabrication on that run, and cooktop cutouts, drainboards and reconnects each carry labour. The calculator on this site prices those individually rather than hiding them in a per-foot number.
